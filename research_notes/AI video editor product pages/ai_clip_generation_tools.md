# AI-first video generation and short-form clip tools: how Runway, Captions, OpusClip, Submagic and Riverside present their product and feature pages (researched 2026-11-04)

Method: live pages fetched on 2026-11-04 through a text extractor (so images, video embeds and button labels drawn as images can be missing), plus OpusClip's help centre and a few 2026 third-party comparisons. runwayml.com now 308-redirects to runway.com. Every vendor figure below is the vendor's own claim and was not independently checked.

## 1. Which product and feature URLs exist for each

### Takeaway
All five run large URL estates, but they split two ways. Submagic and Captions run SEO-style networks of single-feature, "tool" and use-case pages. Runway, Riverside and OpusClip pair a few flagship product pages (agent, MCP, API, model pages) with app or demo deep links. Only Submagic runs a full "vs" and alternatives network.

- KeyFly's Product page could be one deep hub that does the flagship job well. SEO feature pages can follow later.
- Agent, MCP and API pages are now standard: Runway /mcp and /product/agent, OpusClip /mcp and /api, Riverside /agents and /mcp, Submagic /api. KeyFly already runs inside ChatGPT and Claude and works with external agents, so it has a credible agent and MCP story to tell.

### Gaps
- No sitemap.xml files were fetched, so the URL lists come from navigation and footers and may be incomplete (OpusClip especially, since its footer was not captured).

## 2. Section structure of the main product and feature pages, and how features are grouped

### Takeaway
Most pages follow one template: hero, logos or metrics, 3–6 capability blocks, a 3-step "how it works", use cases, testimonials, FAQ, closing CTA. The interesting variations are a working editor widget in the hero (Captions), a prompt-to-clip demo with genre tabs (OpusClip ClipAnything), an agent "team" layout (Riverside), and a model catalogue (Runway). Features are grouped mostly by capability. Workflow stage is used only by Riverside and in "how it works" strips.

## 3. Proof devices

### Takeaway
Scale numbers (20M, 60M, 4M) and logo walls dominate. Creator-results metrics (OpusClip), benchmark tables (OpusClip ClipAnything), review badges (Submagic), Hollywood and TV case studies (Runway) and multi-style demos (Captions) are the stronger, more specific devices. Few pages offer true before/after or interactive demos. Captions' working upload widget and erase before/after are the exceptions.

### Cited Findings
- **Runway:** "Used by 60m+ creatives" (homepage) and "60M+ users globally" (/product); customer stories on House of David (Amazon), History Channel's "Life After People", Under Armour and Chime; no logo wall or quotes in the extracted text; self-claims such as Gen-4.5 as "The world's best video model" — [Runway home](https://runway.com/); [Runway /product](https://runway.com/product)
- **Captions homepage:**
  - Metrics: "20M current users", "100+ languages", "100+ caption styles".

- The proof that persuades in this category is concrete: outcome metrics tied to named creators, benchmark tables and named production case studies. Generic user counts are table stakes. - Interactive demos are rare. Only Captions has a working upload widget and style picker; OpusClip's genre tabs only switch videos. KeyFly's live prompt box and scroll-played demos already exceed most of these.

### Gaps
- Embedded video lengths and content, and whether any demo shows a real unedited source next to its output, could not be checked from text extraction.
- No independent verification of any user-count or performance metric was found.

## 5. How AI is explained: decisions shown, steering, undo, or black box

### Takeaway
Explainability is thin across the category. OpusClip and Captions show a virality score, a number from 0–99 used to rank clips, but no per-clip reasons. Steering through prompts or chat is now universal. Approval gates and undo are rarely stated; Riverside /agents ("nothing publishes without approval") is the strongest. No page shows an AI "decision log" with reasons and a revert, which leaves room for KeyFly's "You make the calls" idea.

### Cited Findings
- **OpusClip virality score:** a 0–99 scale based on four factors (Hook, Flow, Value, Trend), plus prompt relevance for ClipAnything. Clips are sorted by score. Starter and Pro only; free users can't see scores. The help page doesn't say whether sub-scores or written reasons are shown — [OpusClip help](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/virality-score)

There are two modes, Hands-Free (auto genre) and Custom (genre plus a typed prompt), and a linked prompt manual. No trimming or timeline controls are described — [ClipAnything](https://www.opus.pro/clipanything)

- **OpusClip 4.0:**
  - "Recipes": a saved, reusable editing setup.
  - "AI Producer": a ChatGPT or Claude plugin that edits footage to match a style you show it.
  - [OpusClip 4.0 post](https://www.opus.pro/blog/opusclip-4-0)
- **Captions /features/create-clips:**
  - "Virality ranking" with screenshot scores from 98 down; no reasons.
  - Steering by asking the chat editor for more clips by topic, speaker or tone.
  - Manual trimming before export.

- **Captions /features/edit-with-ai:**
  - Says the AI "analyzes your video and suggests updates".

  - Undo is not mentioned.
  - [Edit with AI](https://www.captions.ai/features/edit-with-ai)
- **Submagic:** a virality score is mentioned once on the Magic Clips page, in the "Magic" step; reasons are not explained; the model is described as "trained on social media content". The homepage names no models and doesn't explain choices — [Magic Clips](https://www.submagic.co/features/magic-clips); [Submagic home](https://www.submagic.co/)
- **Riverside /magic-clips:** the AI "picks" highlights with no scoring or reasons shown, but control is detailed (duration, ratio, layout, focus speaker or keywords, transcript editing, saved presets) — [Magic Clips](https://riverside.com/magic-clips)

  - Agents propose edit plans and clips after recording and report back in chat.

- **Runway:** AI is explained as a catalogue of models. Control is through text instructions, Aleph 2.0's edit-one-frame-propagate-to-all, and node-based Workflows. There is no clip-reason concept — [Runway /product](https://runway.com/product)
- **Third-party view:** a review aggregator reports that reviewers see OpusClip's score as a shortlist aid, not a reliable predictor — [datastudios](https://www.datastudios.org/post/opus-clip-clipanything-video-repurposing-virality-scoring-and-pricing) (via search summary; not fetched directly)

### Inferences
- The category's "transparency" is a single opaque number. A page that shows why the AI chose each cut (e.g. "kept: strongest hook, 0:42–0:58"), with a one-click revert, would clearly beat it. KeyFly's app already has Revert and its homepage has the Inspector concept.
- Steering has converged on "chat or prompt to edit" (Captions, Riverside Co-Creator, Runway Agent, OpusClip AI Producer), so prompt-based editing alone is no longer a differentiator. The differentiators are visible decisions, granular sensitivity controls (Riverside) and approval gates.
- Agent hand-off levels (Riverside: "choose the level of handoff") are a useful framing for a KeyFly Product page.

### Gaps
- Whether OpusClip shows the Hook, Flow, Value and Trend sub-scores in its UI could not be confirmed from the help page.
- Undo and version history were not described on any fetched marketing page. In-app behaviour was not tested.

## 8. Distinctive ideas worth beating, and what feels weak, cluttered or generic

### Takeaway
The ideas worth beating:
- Captions' live editor widget and same-video-in-three-styles demo.
- OpusClip's prompt-to-clip genre demos, benchmark and "Anything vs Basic" tables, and copyable MCP prompts.
- Riverside's agent "team" with approval gates and its "AI that plays by your rules" control framing.
- Runway's single-task "apps" and edit-one-frame-propagate-to-all.

The common weaknesses: catalogue-style grids, inconsistent or unverifiable metrics, opaque AI scores, generic SEO FAQs, and broken or placeholder links.

### Cited Findings
**Strong ideas**
- Captions: a hero with a working upload and style picker (20 styles), a "same video, three editing styles" demo, and a before/after "Erase" of burned-in captions billed as exclusive — [Captions home](https://www.captions.ai/); [Create clips](https://www.captions.ai/features/create-clips)
- OpusClip ClipAnything: genre tabs with demo videos, 11 persona demo pages, Hands-Free vs Custom modes, an "Anything vs Basic" table and a benchmark table — [ClipAnything](https://www.opus.pro/clipanything)
- OpusClip MCP: "Try it with one prompt" with copyable prompts, a builder template gallery, and pricing transparency for agents (balance checks, 429 retry headers) — [OpusClip MCP](https://www.opus.pro/mcp)
- OpusClip 4.0: "Recipes" (saved editing setups), "Every format" (one edit to highlight, extended cut and lo-fi update) and "AI Producer" — [OpusClip 4.0 post](https://www.opus.pro/blog/opusclip-4-0)
- Riverside /agents:
  - Four role-named agents.
  - Agents that act unprompted once recording stops.
  - A phone notification and approval mockup.
  - "Choose the level of handoff".
  - [Agents](https://riverside.com/agents)
- Riverside /ai: sections framed by workflow stage and by user control — [Riverside AI](https://riverside.com/ai)
- Runway: nine single-purpose "apps" (Relight Scene, Change Time of Day and others), Aleph 2.0's frame edit that propagates through the video, node-based Workflows, and production-grade case studies — [Runway /product](https://runway.com/product)

**Weak, cluttered or generic**
- **Submagic Magic Clips:** seven extra use-case cards link to "#"; several FAQ entries cover generic AI topics, not Submagic — [Magic Clips](https://www.submagic.co/features/magic-clips)
- **Submagic /vs:** logo cards only, with no criteria — [Submagic /vs](https://www.submagic.co/vs)
- **OpusClip homepage:**
  - Testimonials repeated several times.
  - Two creator entries appeared mismatched with their images.
  - 97% vs 99% accuracy, and credits vs minutes, between the visible text and the structured data.
  - [OpusClip home](https://www.opus.pro/)
- **OpusClip ClipAnything:** unsupported "award-winning producers" and "first-ever" claims, and a benchmark it does not win on every row — [ClipAnything](https://www.opus.pro/clipanything)
- **Riverside /magic-clips:** about 17 sections and 5 near-identical "Get started" bands; "Magic Clips. Real results." shows creators with no results; "Contact support" links to "#" — [Magic Clips](https://riverside.com/magic-clips)
- **Riverside footers:** still show "© 2024" while the content references 2026 — [Agents](https://riverside.com/agents)
- **Runway /product:** a catalogue stacked to roughly 50 items (12 models, 8 cards, 10 chips, 9 apps, 3 stories, 3 news), with third-party models given equal billing next to Runway's own — [Runway /product](https://runway.com/product)
- **Captions homepage:** no subhead, no testimonials, and press logos with no linked articles — [Captions home](https://www.captions.ai/)

### Inferences
- **The tour:** KeyFly's Product page can beat these by being one guided, interactive tour of a single project from prompt to export, with no catalogue grids.
- **Visible decisions:** show the AI's decisions with reasons and a Revert. That beats the opaque virality score of OpusClip and Captions.
- **Steering:** show "edit by asking" next to precise manual controls. That combines Captions' and Riverside's chat steering with Riverside's control framing.
- **Professional hand-off:** make it a hero feature (FCPXML, EDL, OTIO, Package for editing), since none of the five makes it central.
- **Proof:** KeyFly cannot invent metrics, logos or testimonials. It should prove things the way Captions' style demo and OpusClip's prompt demos do (something visitors can try or watch), and add real app footage once it exists.
- **Copyable prompts:** a one-click prompt list for agents, like OpusClip's MCP page, maps directly onto KeyFly's existing "keyfly:prompt" gallery and its ChatGPT and Claude story.

### Gaps
- Visual design quality, motion and page performance could not be judged from text extraction. A browser-based visual audit would be needed.
- Third-party 2025–2026 reviews found were mostly written by competing vendors (bytecap, thumbmagic, revid and others). No independent design critique of these pages was found.
