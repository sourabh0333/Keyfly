# Product / features pages of browser-and-desktop AI video editors (VEED, Kapwing, CapCut, Canva, Adobe Premiere + Firefly)

Research date: 2026-11-04. Pages were read through a text fetcher (WebFetch), so the layout descriptions come from page text and markup, not screenshots. Animation, autoplay behaviour and visual hierarchy could not be observed directly. **Every canva.com page returned HTTP 403** (the video editor, the features page, the CapCut-alternative page and the newsroom). A request that set a browser user agent to get past the block was refused by the session's safety classifier as bot-check circumvention, and no further attempt was made. Canva findings therefore come only from search snippets of canva.com pages and third-party reviews, and are marked as such.

## 1. Which product/feature URLs exist for each vendor?

All five use a hub-and-spoke SEO structure: a tools or features hub that links to hundreds of single-tool landing pages. VEED and Kapwing are the most extreme (several hundred long-tail pages each). ## 2. Hub page structure: sections in order, how features are grouped, how many are shown

### Takeaway
Two families. **Directories** (VEED /tools, Kapwing /tools, CapCut /tools) are card grids grouped by capability (Kapwing also by output type), with 40 to 400+ entries and almost no narrative. **Narrative feature pages** (Adobe features, Kapwing AI, VEED AI agent) run hero → demo → grouped benefits → how-to steps → proof → FAQ → closing CTA. Only Adobe groups by workflow stage (Editing / Effects / Title / Color / Audio / Exporting).

## 8. Distinctive ideas worth beating, and what feels weak, cluttered or generic

### Takeaway
The ideas worth beating are Adobe's workflow-stage tabs, VEED's "brand, not AI" positioning and single repeated CTA, Kapwing's dense honest-looking specs (aspect ratios, formats, devices), and Firefly's multi-model hub with autoplay clip carousels. The weaknesses are tool-farm clutter, repetitive grids, self-contradicting numbers, stale proof, third-party model names as headlines, and the absence of anything you can actually try on the page.

### Cited Findings
- **Worth beating:**
  - Adobe's 7-tab hub grouped by stage, with about 6 cards per tab and a user-guide link after each. — [Premiere features](https://www.adobe.com/products/premiere/features.html)
  - VEED's homepage bento, "Generate videos that look like your brand, not AI", and its video-type tabs (Explainer, Product Demo, Ad…). — [VEED home](https://www.veed.io/)
  - Kapwing's "What's different" list (Easy, Free, Collaborative, Online, No spam or ads, AI-Powered) and its full spec FAQ. — [Kapwing AI Video Editor](https://www.kapwing.com/ai/copilot); [Kapwing AI](https://www.kapwing.com/ai/overview)
  - Firefly's model-by-model subsections with "Try … in Firefly" and its explicit commercial-safety claim. — [Firefly AI video generator](https://www.adobe.com/products/firefly/features/ai-video-generator.html)
  - Adobe's AI page leads with three hero AI capabilities, then "even more" and "more" tiers: a clear ranking of importance. — [Premiere AI video editing](https://www.adobe.com/products/premiere/ai-video-editing.html)
- **Weak, cluttered or generic:**
  - VEED's tools hub is a flat A–Z of several hundred links with no category headings. — [VEED Tools](https://www.veed.io/tools)
  - Kapwing's /tools mixes about 400 A–Z entries with novelty "Just For Fun" pages (Cartoonify, Unofficial Netflix Wrapped). — [Kapwing Tools](https://www.kapwing.com/tools)
  - Kapwing contradicts itself on one page (9 vs 18 AI models; 40+ vs 100+ languages). — [Kapwing AI](https://www.kapwing.com/ai/overview)
  - VEED's agent page says "No credit card required" while its FAQ says the playground needs a subscription. — [VEED AI Video Agent](https://www.veed.io/tools/video-gpt/ai-video-agent)
  - CapCut Desktop's H1 sells a third-party model (Seedance 2.5) and its footer names a different version (2.0); its testimonials date from 2023; and its suite page repeats "Try for free" about 28 times. — [CapCut Desktop](https://www.capcut.com/tools/desktop-video-editor); [CapCut creative suite](https://www.capcut.com/creative-suite)
  - Adobe's features hub has no video, logos or testimonials and is heavy with promotions (countdown timers, "Save now"). — [Premiere features](https://www.adobe.com/products/premiere/features.html)
  - VEED and Kapwing pages duplicate testimonial carousels in the markup, and VEED's agent page repeats the same four testimonials as its collaboration page. — [VEED AI Video Agent](https://www.veed.io/tools/video-gpt/ai-video-agent); [VEED Video Collaboration](https://www.veed.io/tools/video-collaboration)
  - Reviewers say Canva lacks chroma key, colour grading and export control, and caps AI clips at 8 seconds: "a design tool with video features layered on top". — [Fluxnote review](https://fluxnote.io/guides/canva-video-editor-review)

### Inferences
- **Opportunities for KeyFly's Product page:**
  1. Group by workflow stage like Adobe, but make each stage a working demo, not a static card. No rival offers in-page interaction, and KeyFly already has these components (prompt box, edit-with-words, every format, on brand, hand-off).
  2. Lead with KeyFly's own differentiators, not model names: the AI director writing storyboard, voice-over and motion graphics; refining by asking; bring-your-own agents without AI minutes; timeline hand-off to Final Cut Pro, Resolve, Premiere and Avid.
  3. Publish one honest spec block (formats, aspect ratios, platforms, export targets) in the clean, checkable style of Kapwing's FAQ, with no contradictions.
  4. Use one primary CTA repeated at three fixed points, like VEED, and avoid CapCut's flood and Adobe's promotional clutter.
  5. Because KeyFly cannot use logos or user counts, use transparency as proof: "Illustrative" labels, facts read from the app, and a link to try the real app.

### Gaps
- No third-party UX audit of these product pages was found. The "weak or cluttered" judgements above are drawn from the page structures recorded here.
- Canva could not be assessed first-hand.
